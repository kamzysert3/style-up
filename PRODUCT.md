# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

adaptive

## Users

The developer, using it privately on their phone — usually in front of a mirror, minutes before heading out — to decide whether an outfit is actually good or just feels familiar. The core emotional job is *trust*: "can I believe this score?" A secondary audience of other outfit-indecisive people is planned for later, but nothing in v1 is designed for them.

## Product Purpose

StyleUp takes a photo of what you're wearing and returns an honest 0-100 score with specific, garment-level feedback and one concrete swap that would improve it. Success for M1 is calibration: the user agrees with (or is within ±5 of) the score on at least 16 of 20 real outfits. If the score isn't trusted, nothing downstream (swaps, wardrobe, try-on) matters.

## Positioning

Honest score + one next step — a critic that learns your taste.

## Brand Personality

Honest / direct, calm / minimal, editorial / fashion-forward. The voice is a sharp stylist who respects your time: specific ("the olive chinos break cleanly over the boots"), never generic ("looks great!"). Visually: photography-first, quiet surfaces, confident type — restraint that lets the photo and the score be the event.

## Anti-references

- **AI-slop aesthetic** — rainbow gradients, glassmorphism, glowing blobs, the generic "AI app" look.
- **Girly / teen magazine** — bubblegum pinks, sparkles, overly cute decoration.
- Not explicitly selected but implied by personality: enterprise dashboards and hypebeast/bro energy are off-lane too.

## Design Principles

- The score is the hero — nothing on a screen competes with it.
- Every result delivers one concrete next step, stated as a garment swap.
- Specificity over fluff — name the garment, name the change; ban vague praise in copy and in model feedback alike.
- Editorial restraint — generous whitespace, real photography, one type family in strong weights.
- Trust through consistency — the same outfit should score the same way tomorrow.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Solid defaults: body contrast ≥ 4.5:1, large tap targets (≥ 44pt), readable dynamic type, reduced-motion respected (no essential info carried by animation alone), color never the sole carrier of score meaning (always paired with the number).
